How to Fix the "Input type and weight type should be the same" Error in PyTorch

Source: Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 votes

I still remember the first time I saw this RuntimeError pop up on my terminal. I had just spent an entire afternoon meticulously building a custom CNN architecture, and I was so ready to see that loss curve start ticking down on my shiny new GPU. Instead, I spent three hours staring at a wall of red text because I’d completely forgotten that my data was still chilling on the CPU while my model had already moved to the GPU. It’s one of those classic PyTorch "gotchas" that makes you want to throw your keyboard through the window, but once you get the hang of it, it becomes second nature to spot.

The reason this happens is actually quite logical once you peek under the hood of how PyTorch manages memory. PyTorch is extremely strict about where your tensors live. If your model parameters are loaded onto the GPU (which they are when you call .to(device)), every single piece of data passing through that model must also reside on the GPU. When you see that error, it means your model is waiting for data on the device, but your input tensor is still sitting in your system's RAM. They aren't in the same house, so they can't communicate, and the framework throws a fit.

Fixing this is straightforward, but it’s easy to miss if you’re moving fast. You don't need to move the entire dataset to the GPU at once—that’ll just crash your VRAM. Instead, you need to manually move each batch of data to the correct device inside your training loop right before you feed it into the model. Here is exactly how I handle it in my scripts:


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data, labels in dataloader:
    # This is the line that saves the day
    inputs = data.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # ... rest of your training logic

Once you add those .to(device) calls inside your loop, the error disappears instantly. I’ve found that the best way to prevent this from ever happening again is to get in the habit of writing a helper function or a simple wrapper for your training step. If you standardize your data loading pipeline so that your device movement happens automatically inside your Dataset or DataLoader wrapper, you’ll never have to worry about manual moves again.

Hope this clears up the confusion and saves you from a few hours of debugging. Happy coding, and may your training runs always be error-free!

PyTorch 報錯解密：別讓 GPU 和 CPU 打架啦！

來源：Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 票

記得剛開始碰 PyTorch 那陣子，為了趕一個死線模型訓練，我興奮地把顯卡設定好，想說這次終於能體驗 GPU 加速的快感了。結果程式一跑，螢幕上直接噴出一長串紅通通的 RuntimeError: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same。我當時盯著螢幕看了十分鐘，心裡想說：「老兄，我不是已經把模型丟到 cuda 了嗎？你到底在鬧什麼脾氣？」那種好不容易把環境架好，卻因為一個參數沒對上而卡住的無力感，真的是差點讓我直接把鍵盤給砸了。

其實這背後的邏輯說穿了很簡單，就是 PyTorch 這傢伙非常嚴格，它要求模型參數（Weights）和我們餵進去的資料（Input）必須待在同一個「房間」裡。當我們執行 model.to(device) 時，模型的權重確實搬到了 GPU（cuda）上，但我們在 DataLoader 讀取出來的資料，預設還是躺在系統記憶體（CPU）裡。當兩者在運算層相遇時，一個在顯卡、一個在記憶體，就像雞同鴨講，PyTorch 當然會直接罷工報錯。

要解決這個問題，其實只要在迴圈裡多加一個簡單的動作，告訴資料也要一起搬過去就行了。我通常會直接在迴圈一開始的地方，把讀進來的資料也透過 .to(device) 送進去。程式碼長得會像這樣：


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # 這裡就是關鍵！把 inputs 和 labels 一起搬到你指定的 device 上
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # 接下來的 loss 計算也要記得把 labels 也放同一個位置喔

為了避免以後再犯這種低級錯誤，我養成了一個習慣：在寫 DataLoader 的處理邏輯時，直接寫一個小小的 helper function 或者統一在迴圈開頭做搬移。現在我只要看到那個 RuntimeError，腦袋裡第一個念頭就是「啊，肯定又是哪邊忘了搬資料了」。

反正這就是 PyTorch 訓練時的日常啦，習慣了就好，下次遇到這種紅字別慌，把它們都趕到同一個顯卡上就沒事了！
