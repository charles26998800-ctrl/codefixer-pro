Solving the "RuntimeError: Input type and weight type should be the same" in PyTorch

Source: Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 votes

I remember the first time I ran into this error, I was deep into a late-night training session for a computer vision project. I’d spent about four hours debugging a data augmentation pipeline, only to have the training loop crash on the very first batch with this cryptic type mismatch error. I stared at my screen for twenty minutes, convinced my GPU drivers were corrupted or that I’d somehow broken PyTorch itself, before realizing I was just being a classic "forgetful dev" who hadn't moved the input data to the right device.

The reason this happens is actually quite straightforward once you stop panicking. PyTorch is very strict about where your tensors live. You see, when you call model.to(device), you’re successfully moving your model's weights to the GPU (the cuda device), but your training loop is still pulling fresh data from your DataLoader directly into your system RAM (the cpu device). Because the model is sitting on your graphics card expecting GPU-resident tensors, and you're feeding it CPU-resident tensors, PyTorch throws an error because it can't perform math operations across two different memory spaces. It’s essentially like trying to plug a European power cord into a US outlet—the hardware just isn't speaking the same language.

To fix this, you have to explicitly move your input tensors and labels to the same device as your model during every iteration of your training loop. I usually handle this right after I unpack the data from my loader. You don't need to overcomplicate it; just call .to(device) on your tensors before they hit the model. Here is how my training loop looks now:


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # Move the inputs and labels to the device before passing them to the model
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # ... rest of your training logic

If you want to prevent this from ruining your flow in the future, my best advice is to write a small helper function or a custom Dataset wrapper that handles device assignment automatically, or just make it a habit to check your device placement in your training boilerplate. I personally keep a device variable defined at the very top of my training script and treat it like a constant, so I never have to guess where my tensors are supposed to go.

Hopefully, this saves you the headache it caused me back in the day. Happy training, and may your loss curves always trend downward!

被 PyTorch 的裝置錯置搞到快瘋掉？RuntimeError: Input type mismatch 解決指南

來源：Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 票

還記得剛開始接觸 PyTorch 的時候，有次為了趕論文進度，好不容易寫完模型、準備好資料，滿心期待按下執行鍵，結果螢幕上直接噴出一行紅通通的錯誤訊息：「RuntimeError: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same」。當下我真的差點把鍵盤砸了，心想我明明都寫了 .to(device) 啊，為什麼這個系統還是要這樣折磨我？

其實這個問題的核心邏輯很簡單，就是 PyTorch 是一個非常嚴格的「分組派」，它要求你的模型參數和你的輸入資料，必須待在同一個「房間」裡。當你把模型丟到 GPU (cuda)，但你的資料卻還在 CPU (FloatTensor) 裡面時，兩邊根本無法對話。這就像是你在寫程式時，一邊用繁體中文溝通，另一邊卻堅持要講火星文，運算單元根本無法執行矩陣乘法，最後只能無奈地拋出錯誤，讓你的程式直接冷掉。

要解決這個問題，其實只要在訓練迴圈裡多加一個動作就行了。當你從 dataloader 取出資料時，別忘了把這些輸入資料也「推」到你定義好的 device 上。我後來修改程式碼的方式，就是直接在迴圈內多加一行 .to(device)，看起來會像這樣：


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # 這裡就是關鍵，要把 inputs 和 labels 都送到同一個裝置上
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # 接下來就是計算 loss 等等...

最痛苦的莫過於，你以為自己已經設定好 model.to(device) 就萬事大吉，結果卻忽略了資料流才是那個沒跟上的傢伙，這種「明明設定了卻還是錯」的挫折感，真的會讓人懷疑人生。

為了避免以後再被這種低級錯誤卡住，我現在習慣寫一個簡單的工具函式，或者在定義 DataLoader 的時候直接封裝好資料處理邏輯。最推薦的做法是，在你的訓練函數開頭就檢查一遍，確保所有進入模型的 tensor 都經過 to(device) 的洗禮，甚至可以直接寫一個小小的 Helper function 來處理所有資料的搬運，這樣就再也不用擔心漏掉哪一個變數了。

希望大家下次看到這個紅字時，別跟我當初一樣慌，冷靜下來把資料送過去就沒事啦，祝各位訓練模型順利！
