Solving the "Input type and weight type should be the same" Error in PyTorch

Source: Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 votes

I still remember the first time I set up a distributed training job for a computer vision project, feeling pretty proud of my hardware utilization until I hit a wall with this exact error. I spent nearly three hours debugging my data loader, convinced that my GPU drivers were somehow corrupted or that my CUDA installation had magically vanished overnight. It was one of those classic "Friday afternoon" moments where I was staring at the screen, questioning my life choices, only to realize I’d simply forgotten to move a single tensor to the device.

At its core, this error is PyTorch’s way of telling you that your model and your data aren't playing in the same sandbox. When you call model.to(device), you are essentially telling the model to pack its bags and move into your GPU's VRAM. However, PyTorch doesn’t automatically move your incoming data batches to the GPU for you. If your model parameters are living on the GPU (as torch.cuda.FloatTensor) and your input data is still sitting in your system's RAM (as torch.FloatTensor), the math simply can't happen. It’s like trying to perform a surgery where the doctor is in the operating room, but the patient is still stuck in the parking lot.

Fixing this is straightforward once you realize the disconnect. Within your training loop, you need to explicitly push every tensor that touches the model onto the same device. I usually define my device at the start of my script and then make sure my inputs and labels are moved during the iteration. Here is the standard way I handle it to ensure everything stays in sync:


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # Explicitly move inputs and labels to the device
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # ... rest of your training logic

If you find yourself writing this over and over, you can prevent this headache by creating a small helper function or a custom wrapper around your data loader. Honestly, the most reliable way to avoid this is to get into the habit of writing a .to(device) call immediately after unpacking your batch. It’s second nature to me now, but I still keep that device variable clearly visible at the top of my training functions so I don't have to hunt for it when things go sideways.

Hopefully, this saves you the three hours of frustration I had to endure. Happy coding, and may your GPU memory never overflow!

當 PyTorch 跟我鬧脾氣：RuntimeError: Input type and weight type should be the same

來源：Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 票

我還記得剛開始玩 PyTorch 的那幾天，為了趕在截止日期前跑出訓練結果，我在實驗室熬了一個通宵。好不容易把模型架構寫好、資料集也處理完，按下執行鍵的那一刻，心情簡直比準備開獎還緊張，結果螢幕上瞬間噴出一大串紅通通的錯誤訊息，告訴我 Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same。那一刻真的心臟漏跳一拍，看著終端機上那行冷冰冰的字，我當下只想把鍵盤摔了，畢竟明明都寫了 .to(device) 啊，為什麼還要這樣搞我？

後來我冷靜下來檢查，才發現這根本是 PyTorch 的「基本款」坑。簡單來說，PyTorch 對於資料的「住址」非常講究。你雖然把模型搬到了 GPU 上，但你的資料（也就是 inputs）還懶洋洋地待在 CPU 的記憶體裡。當模型準備好要運算時，它發現自己拿著 GPU 的武器，卻要處理 CPU 送來的原料，這兩邊根本對不上頻率，當然會直接罷工報錯。這就像是你要去台北參加會議，卻把筆電忘在高雄家裡一樣，兩邊不同步，事情當然辦不成。

解決這個問題其實非常簡單，既然模型已經在 GPU 上了，我們在讀取資料時，也必須順手把這些資料「搬運」到同一個裝置上。我當時解決的方式是在迴圈裡面加了一行關鍵代碼，直接把 inputs 和 labels 同步到 device：


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # 這裡就是關鍵！要把資料也丟到跟模型同一個 device
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # 接下來的 loss 計算也要記得把 labels 放在同一邊

後來為了避免以後又因為這種低級錯誤浪費時間，我養成了一個習慣：只要定義了 device 變數，後續在處理張量時，我會直接寫一個小小的 Helper Function，或者在資料讀取完的當下就強制轉移。只要養成「資料進來，先確認住址」的反射動作，這種錯誤基本上就不會再出現了。

希望這招能幫你避開那個熬夜崩潰的夜晚，祝你的模型 loss 降得飛快，訓練過程一切順利！
