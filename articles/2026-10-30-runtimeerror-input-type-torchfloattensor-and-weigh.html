How to Fix "RuntimeError: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same"

Source: Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 votes

I still remember the first time I set up a GPU training loop in PyTorch, feeling like a total wizard until this exact error slapped me in the face. I spent nearly three hours debugging my model architecture, convinced I had a faulty layer definition, only to realize I’d completely forgotten that my data was still chilling on the CPU while my model was living its best life on the GPU. It’s one of those classic "welcome to deep learning" moments that makes you want to throw your laptop out the window, but once you understand the underlying mechanism, it becomes second nature to avoid.

The reason you're seeing this error is actually quite straightforward: PyTorch is incredibly strict about hardware locality. When you move your model to the GPU using .to(device), you are effectively telling your graphics card to host the weight tensors. However, your input data from the DataLoader defaults to the CPU (the FloatTensor). When you pass those CPU-based inputs into a GPU-based model, PyTorch throws a fit because it can't perform math operations across two different memory spaces. It’s essentially trying to bridge a gap that doesn't exist, and the framework stops execution immediately to prevent a silent performance disaster.

To fix this, you need to be explicit about moving your input tensors to the same device as your model inside your training loop. I don’t rely on magic or global settings for this; I just explicitly call .to(device) on my inputs and labels as soon as they emerge from the dataloader. Here is how I structure my loop to ensure everything stays in sync:


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for inputs, labels in dataloader:
    # This is the line that saves the day
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # ... rest of your training logic

If you’re working with custom datasets or complex pipelines, it's easy to lose track of where your tensors are. My best piece of advice to prevent this from ever happening again is to define a small helper function or a consistent variable name for your target device and stick to it religiously throughout your script. If you make it a habit to call .to(device) the moment you pull data out of your iterable, you'll save yourself from ever having to stare at this specific traceback again.

Hope this clears up the confusion and gets your training loop back on track. Happy coding!

救命！PyTorch 報錯：Input 和 Weight 型別不一致的崩潰時刻

來源：Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 票

記得剛開始碰 PyTorch 的時候，我熬夜跑一個模型訓練，好不容易搞定環境變數、配置好顯卡，按下 Run 的瞬間心裡還想著「終於可以去睡覺了」。結果螢幕沒跳出預期的 loss 下降，反而丟給我一個大大的 RuntimeError: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same。那一刻我真的差點把鍵盤砸了，心想我明明都寫了 .to(device) 啊，為什麼顯卡還是在跟我鬧脾氣？

這其實是 PyTorch 初學者最容易掉進去的坑。簡單來說，PyTorch 對資料的擺放位置非常龜毛，它要求「運算中的所有參與者」必須待在同一個地方。當你把模型透過 model.to(device) 移到了 GPU（cuda）上，但你的訓練資料（inputs）還留在 CPU（FloatTensor）裡頭時，PyTorch 就會像個強迫症患者一樣跳出來報錯，因為它沒辦法讓一個在顯卡裡的權重去跟一個在記憶體裡的資料做矩陣運算。

我當時花了一個小時檢查模型定義、檢查顯卡驅動，最後才發現問題出在迴圈裡。解決方法其實超級簡單，你只需要在讀取資料的當下，順手把資料也「送過去」就好了。我通常會習慣這樣寫：


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # 關鍵就在這裡，把 inputs 和 labels 都丟到跟 model 同一個 device
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # 接下來的 loss 計算也要記得確認是否都在同一個 device 上

如果你想徹底擺脫這個惡夢，我給你的建議是，以後寫訓練迴圈時，養成一個「物件導向」的防禦性習慣。與其每次都手動 .to(device)，不如把這件事封裝在你的 Dataset 處理邏輯，或者乾脆在寫 DataLoader 的時候就多確認一遍。我後來都會習慣在程式開頭定義一個 to_device 的輔助函式，不管之後資料結構多複雜，通通丟進去轉一遍，這樣就算以後換了更複雜的架構，也不會再被這種基礎錯誤卡住。

搞定這個之後，就能安心去睡覺或是繼續下一個實驗了，祝你的模型 Loss 都能順利下降！
