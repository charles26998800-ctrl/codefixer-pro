Solving the "RuntimeError: Input type and weight type should be the same" in PyTorch

Source: Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 votes

I still remember the first time I encountered this error during a late-night training session for a computer vision project. I had spent three hours fine-tuning my model architecture, feeling like a genius, only to hit this wall the second I triggered the training loop. I stared at my terminal for twenty minutes, absolutely convinced I’d broken my GPU, before realizing I was just a victim of a classic mismatch between my data and my hardware.

The reason this happens is actually quite straightforward once you step back: PyTorch is incredibly strict about hardware parity. When you move your model to the GPU using model.to(device), you are effectively uploading the model weights into the memory of your graphics card. However, your data—the input tensors coming out of your DataLoader—are still sitting comfortably in your system's CPU RAM. When you feed that CPU-based tensor into a GPU-based model, PyTorch throws this runtime error because it can’t perform mathematical operations across different memory spaces. It’s essentially the software equivalent of trying to plug a European power adapter into an American outlet; they just don't speak the same language.

To fix this, you need to explicitly move your input data onto the same device as your model during each iteration of your training loop. I’ve found that the cleanest way to do this is to keep your device defined globally and push your tensors to that device before they ever touch the model. Here is the snippet I use in my projects:


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for inputs, labels in dataloader:
    # Move the inputs and labels to the device before the forward pass
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # Proceed with your loss calculation...

By adding those two .to(device) lines, you ensure that the tensors are transferred to the GPU memory before the model performs its forward pass. It’s a simple fix, but it saves you a world of headache.

The best way to prevent this from creeping back into your codebase is to stop hardcoding device movements inside your loops whenever possible. I like to write a small helper function or a custom wrapper around my data loading process that handles the device transfer automatically. If you make it a habit to check the device of your inputs as soon as they exit the DataLoader, you’ll never see this error again.

Hopefully, this saves you the three hours of hair-pulling that it originally cost me. Happy coding and may your gradients always converge!

PyTorch 報錯 `RuntimeError: Input type (torch.FloatTensor) and weight type (torch.cuda.FloatTensor) should be the same`，這坑我踩過！

來源：Stack Overflow ↗ · 202 票

那天深夜，我已經連著趕了三天進度，模型終於訓練到一半了，螢幕卻突然跳出這一行紅通通的錯誤訊息。那一刻我真的差點崩潰，明明寫程式的時候都已經把 model.to(device) 加上去了，為什麼 PyTorch 還在跟我鬧脾氣，說我的輸入資料跟權重的類型不對？看著螢幕上那行像是在嘲笑我的錯誤代碼，我當時真的是滿頭問號，甚至懷疑是不是顯卡離家出走了。

其實，這問題說穿了就是「溝通障礙」。PyTorch 非常嚴格，它要求你的模型參數（Weights）和你的輸入資料（Inputs）必須待在同一個地方——要嘛都在 CPU，要嘛都在 GPU。當你把模型丟到 GPU 後，你的輸入資料（通常是從 DataLoader 讀出來的）卻還留在系統記憶體（CPU）裡，這兩個「不同世界」的東西硬要湊在一起運算，PyTorch 當然會直接罷工報錯。這真的很痛苦，尤其是在訓練腳本跑了半小時才噴出這個錯，那種「早知道就先檢查一下」的悔恨感，真的是每個工程師的惡夢。

要解決這個問題其實很簡單，就是要在每個迴圈裡，把讀進來的資料也主動趕去跟模型會合。我通常會直接在 for 迴圈裡面加一行 .to(device)，確保資料進到模型前已經準備好了。你可以參考我現在習慣的寫法：


device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
model.to(device)

for data in dataloader:
    # 這裡就是關鍵！記得把 inputs 跟 labels 都丟到同一個 device
    inputs, labels = data
    inputs = inputs.to(device)
    labels = labels.to(device)
    
    outputs = model(inputs)
    # ... 後續的 loss 計算等步驟

為了避免下次又因為這種低級錯誤被氣到心臟痛，我現在養成了一個習慣，就是會直接寫一個小小的 Helper Function 或者把裝置設定封裝起來，然後在資料處理的 pipeline 最前端就統一處理。如果你真的怕忘記，甚至可以寫個簡單的 assert 來檢查 inputs.device 是不是跟 next(model.parameters()).device 一樣，這樣一有問題馬上就能在第一步擋下來，不用等到跑完 epoch 才發現崩潰。

希望這招能救你脫離苦海，下次寫訓練腳本時，記得別讓資料在半路上迷路啦！
